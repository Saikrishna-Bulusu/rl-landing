Each found the same way: PX4's estimate against Gazebo's truth, flight by flight.

Found 26–27 Sept, one smoke flight at a time before any batch.

Getting to a clean flight with GPS denied took five fixes, and none of them was in GeoAnchor. One: every GeoAnchor fix was rejected by PX4, because the simulated world said it was in Zurich, PX4's default, while the pictures were of Logan; moving the world's origin to the Logan depot fixed it. Two: on the first real try GeoAnchor accepted one frame in fifteen hundred. The simulated compass disagreed with PX4's own magnetic model by 10 to 20 degrees, and GeoAnchor rotates every picture by the compass heading before matching. With GPS on, PX4 corrects its heading from its motion; so GPS is now switched off only after flying on it for a while, and the drone avoids big turns afterwards. Three: without GPS, height comes from the barometer alone, and it ran two to five metres high in every descent; a downward rangefinder, standard on landing drones, fixed that. Four: in gusty wind the drone blew ten metres off the pad in the last metre and a half, because I had stopped the marker's fixes below three metres after one bad close-up read; now they run to touchdown, and a jump check throws out bad reads. Five: past the crane, the flight loop froze for six seconds waiting for the planner's path search; now it asks without waiting.
