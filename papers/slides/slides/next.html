What is solved, and what is not

Solved

Marker-based landing, now in trees. 20/20 delivered across twenty different forests, 0 crashes, 0.28–0.56 m, with a guardrail that refuses a stranger's marker.

Measured and refuted

That sigma helps a policy. That a learned policy beats a one-line rule. That better localisation delivers more parcels. That obstacles needed better sensing — they needed a bigger marker.

Not solved

Wires, people, moving obstacles. Pads that cannot be repainted. And the descent decision when the clear column is genuinely too narrow.

The cheapest intervention won. A larger square of paint beat stereo-grade obstacle sensing (+7 reward, never significant), beat a new simulator, and beat every learning change measured here. Localisation still matters — halving the error is worth seven times what richer obstacle sensing was — but it is priced in airframes only once trees are present.

Honest limits. Four crashes is a small sample: the crash rate is not independently significant (p = 0.11); its association with column width is (p = 0.007). Trees carry no collision geometry, so a crash is scored geometrically, not by physics.

Method that earned its keep. Vary the environment seed. A fixed layout gives a tight p-value on a conclusion that can be missing a whole failure mode — here, crashes existing at all.
