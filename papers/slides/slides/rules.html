Who else is flying

An air ambulance into Logan Hospital on half of all flights, six delivery drones out of the hub, and people and cars around the pad.

Two worlds, one hypothesis

In the corridor world the helicopter always arrives from the north-west; in the random world, from anywhere. A learner should gain most where past flights reveal a pattern.

Rules are checked on the drone's true position, after a 3 s grace; the ceiling gets no grace.

This slide defines success, because every number that follows is a percentage of clean deliveries. A delivery only counts if the drone lands on the right pad AND never broke a rule on the way. The rules are the real Australian CASA ones for drones like this: stay under 120 m, stay 30 m from people (including the person waiting for the parcel), never fly over anyone, and near a hospital helipad, get out of the way of a helicopter. The near mid-air distance comes from the ASTM standard for drone detect-and-avoid. CASA has no drone-to-drone distance, so 30 m is our own choice and I say so. The two worlds exist to test one idea: learning should help most when something repeats across flights, like a helicopter that always uses the same approach corridor. If the learner gains more in the corridor world than in the random one, that is evidence it is learning a pattern rather than just reacting faster.
