The live test. PX4 flies the drone in Gazebo, a hunter magpie defends its nest in a tree near the pad, and the drone knows about the magpie only through its cameras: the pictures go through the detector and the tracker, and no true bird position is used anywhere. The picture shows the planner's flight and the learner's flight on the same nest, 39 seconds in; each drone has boxed the magpie in one of its side cameras. The table is the scorecard on the same three nests. Handed the true position, the planner delivers all three. With the old six-camera rig it delivered none. With rig v2 and the up camera, planner and learner each delivered five of nine, over three runs where only the landing code changed. That tie is noise: three flights per run cannot separate them, and that is the reason for the next slide. The useful finding is where the strikes happened: in the last one or two metres of the descent, with the magpie coming from behind right after the drone had dodged it once. The rig had seen it. So the problem left is not seeing the bird, it is deciding when to commit to the last few metres, which is exactly the kind of decision learning should be good at.
