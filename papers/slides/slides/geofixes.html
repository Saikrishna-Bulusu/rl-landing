This slide answers the questions you raised about the films, one row each. The strange corner: the old controller aimed at each corner point using the drone's own estimate, which GeoAnchor had a few metres off, so it hooked toward it, braked to a stop, and then PX4 turned the nose the shorter way from where it had ended up, which was 166 degrees to the right. Now the route is flown like a road, with a target 15 metres ahead, slowing into the corner, and the nose turns left, 133 degrees. The aggressive moves had three sources: those corner stops, a hard brake at the moment GPS was switched off, and the planner's twitching; with commands now allowed to change by at most two metres per second each second, PX4's own log shows at most 2.4 metres per second squared and 16 degrees of tilt at cruise. The agitation before the crane was the planner re-joining its old path at a point GeoAnchor's corrections had shifted. The blue path looked ignored because it was drawn where PX4 thought the drone was. The early descent was the old hand-over. The magpie drone's hovering was the learner dodging tree crowns the rig mistook for magpies; below 5 metres it now just lands. And for honesty, the last row: the first re-flight with the higher approach hit the crane, because each plan took two seconds; a faster search fixed it.
