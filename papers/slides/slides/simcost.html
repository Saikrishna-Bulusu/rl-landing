Sai asked why we can only fly a handful of seeds, and whether MuJoCo or PyBullet would fix it. I timed every step on an idle laptop. Every simulated second the drone takes twenty sets of five HD pictures. Making each set look like a real camera, with noise, blur and JPEG, costs about two seconds of laptop time per simulated second, on the CPU. The bird detector costs another 1.2 seconds. Drawing the seven pictures, which come from six cameras because the OAK-D gives both a colour and a depth picture, and passing them along is about a quarter of a second, and physics plus PX4 is never the limit: five to eight copies ran side by side at full speed in the crane flights. So swapping Gazebo's physics for MuJoCo or PyBullet would speed up the part that is already cheap; if the bird is still known only through pictures, those pictures and that detector cost the same in any simulator. What does help, measured: the camera realism on the GPU, and the detector in half precision. Together that is 3.4 down to 1.3 seconds of work per simulated second, and on 369 held-out magpie pictures the detector finds the bird just as often, 90.0 against 90.5 percent, with the same false alarm rate. Running three copies at once, twenty seeds for each decider should take one to two hours instead of about seven; that is an estimate until it is flown. Step two is to use the fast GPU simulator for the thousands of flights and Gazebo to check it on the same nests. Step three, MuJoCo on the GPU, only if that check shows the fast simulator's flying is what is wrong. PyBullet runs only on the CPU and is slower than MuJoCo, so it is not worth it here.
