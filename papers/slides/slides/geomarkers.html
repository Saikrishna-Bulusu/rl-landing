Why the pad has three markers

A ladder of sizes

A name for the pad

Ids 2, 1, 0 say “our pad”. The 15 neighbours carry ids 3–47 and are refused: the red boxes in the films.

The touchdown point

The small one sits at the mat's centre, the spot we score from. It is also a position fix for PX4 in the last metres.

You asked what the three markers are for. They are one pad seen at three distances. A marker can only be read between two heights: from too high it is too few pixels to decode, and from too low it spills off the edge of the camera's picture. So the big 1.80 metre marker, id 2, is read from about 29 metres down to three quarters of a metre; the 1.20 metre one, id 0, from 19 metres to half a metre; and the tiny 15 centimetre one, id 1, from 2.4 metres down to 6 centimetres. Their ranges overlap, so at no height is there nothing to read. Second, the ids are the pad's name: ours are 2, 1 and 0, and the fifteen neighbouring pads use other ids, which is why the films box theirs in red and ignore them. Third, the tiny marker sits exactly at the mat's centre, which is the touchdown point the landing is scored against. Every read of any of the three is also turned into a position fix for PX4, which is what keeps it within ten centimetres once GeoAnchor fades below 50 metres. The picture is the mat as Gazebo paints it, our own texture, so this slide has no imagery restriction.
