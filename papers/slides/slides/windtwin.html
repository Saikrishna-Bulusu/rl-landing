Part four · learning to land in strong wind

A GPU twin of PX4, checked against Gazebo

To practise thousands of gusty landings at once, the x500 and PX4's own controllers (position, attitude, rate, motor mixing) were rebuilt in MuJoCo on the GPU, with Gazebo's wind model beside them. Velocity steps land within 0.02–0.06 s of PX4 in Gazebo; the lean into a steady wind matches to 0.1°.

MJX: 4,096 flights in parallel on one laptop GPU. Gazebo: PX4 SITL with the WindDrag plugin (log-profile wind, turbulence, gusts).

Part four is about wind. The earlier landing rule works in calm air, but in strong gusty wind it waits for a moment when the drone is centred and still, and that moment never comes, so it times out. To learn something better, the learner needs a very large number of practice landings. Gazebo with PX4 is too slow for that, so I rebuilt the drone and PX4's controllers, position, attitude, rate and the motor mixing, in MuJoCo running on the GPU, which flies four thousand drones at once. A twin is only useful if it behaves like the real thing, so this slide is the check. The left two panels are step commands, go north at 3 or 8 metres per second: the dashed green MJX drone lies on top of PX4 in Gazebo. The right panel is how far the drone leans into a steady wind: the green curve, with body drag added, passes through the Gazebo measurements. So what is learned in the twin can be flown on PX4.
