PX4 in Gazebo, rig v2, a 58 m crane moved per seed onto the delivery line; clearance judged on Gazebo's true positions.

Part three moves from the fast simulator to PX4, the real flight software, flying in Gazebo with real camera pictures. The first test is the obstacle the map cannot know about: a 58 metre tower crane put up after the 2023 survey, placed somewhere different on the delivery line for each of ten flights. Trusting the survey alone, the drone crashed into it eight times out of ten; the two passes were luck, half a metre and 0.8 metres. With the voxel map, the drone's depth camera turns what it sees into half-metre cubes, the orange ones on the mast in the picture, and the drone swerves when its look-ahead hits them: no crashes, but it passed as close as one metre. With the local 3D planner, a path search runs through those cubes and the survey every step, about 20 times a second, keeping two metres of padding: no crashes, and never closer than 2.6 metres. The blue ribbon is the path it plans. Sai's call from this was that static things should be planned, not learned: the planner is a hard layer under any decider, and learning is kept for the things that move, like the magpie. One open detail: with this drone the planner passes a little closer than with the old one, a median of 3.4 metres against 4.6, still outside its two-metre padding, and I have not pinned down why.
