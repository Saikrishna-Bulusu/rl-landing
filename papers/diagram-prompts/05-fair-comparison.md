# Diagram 5 — Why "tuning budget" decides who wins

Ask Gemini to draw this. Everything it needs is below; it has no other context.

## Purpose
Show that a comparison between two methods is meaningless unless both got the
same amount of tuning effort. This mistake was made twice in this project, in
opposite directions.

## Draw
Three panels left to right, like a story in three frames.

### Frame 1 - "ROUND 1: unfair to the AI"
Two boxes on a see-saw / balance scale.
- Left box: "Hand-written rule" with 12 small gear icons above it, labelled
  "12 settings tried".
- Right box: "AI method" with 1 gear icon, labelled "settings guessed by hand".
- The scale tips toward the hand-written rule.
- Result banner: "Hand-written rule wins by 11.7 points" with a red stamp
  reading "NOT A FAIR TEST".

### Frame 2 - "THE FIX"
Same two boxes, now both with 12 gear icons.
- Banner: "Give both sides the same 12 tries."

### Frame 3 - "ROUND 2: fair"
- The scale is level.
- Result banner: "They tie: 73.9 vs 74.8 - the gap was the tuning, not the
  method."

## Caption to include
"If you tune one method carefully and the other carelessly, you are measuring
your own effort, not the methods. The first result said the hand-written rule
was better. It wasn't - it had just been tuned harder."

## Style
Simple three-frame comic strip layout. Balance scale metaphor throughout.
