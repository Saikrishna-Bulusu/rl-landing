The first version of that result was twice too big

The model treated min_px = 34 as a cliff: invisible below, perfect above. Flying the pad contradicted it, so the detection curve was re-flown — from 60 m instead of 26 m, which is why nothing below 25 px had ever been sampled and everything under it was extrapolation.

The effect survives at roughly half the size. And 1.80 m is enough: with a realistic budget 1.80 and 2.40 both deliver 100%, while 1.20 plateaus at 69% however long you give it.

The cliff was not the conservative choice — it was wrong in both directions, calling a 1.20 m marker invisible above 19 m when it in fact decodes half the time at 27 m, and certain below it when it is not.

The default was left on the cliff anyway. Under the measured curve the base environment's own guard fails — diving succeeds 100% at low sigma and 98% at high, so the sigma gap the study rests on disappears. A better detector makes that scenario trivial, and re-tuning it is a separate job with its own re-baselining.
