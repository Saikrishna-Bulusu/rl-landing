The fix: hand it the planner's eyes, then let it practise

1 · See what it sees

For each of the 14 manoeuvres, the planner's 20-second forecast of rule breaks, progress and lateness, and its pick.

2 · Start as a copy

Taught by imitation, it matches the planner on 100% of decisions (it was 48% without the forecast): 95.3%.

3 · Practise, keep the best

PPO for 410,000 flights. Every 10 rounds, score it on separate tuning flights; keep the best version.

Identical options driftIn open sky three manoeuvres are the same command. It drifted to “land anyway” and kept the habit near people: violations 0.1% → 2.5%. Now it only sees distinct options.
An anchor stops learningPulling it back toward the planner held it still: 60 rounds at exactly the copy's score. So it roams, and the best version is kept.
One policy, three read-outsIts top choice, its dice, or the odds-weighted average command. The dice blend moves that are not on the menu — 8% “climb” among “hold” is a gentle climb — and the average keeps that without randomness.

Everything the planner sees, the drone has: no information from the future reaches the policy.

This is the fix, in three steps. First, instead of making the network re-invent the planner's 20-second look-ahead, we hand it the planner's forecast for every manoeuvre: how many rule breaks each option leads to, how much progress it makes, whether it will be late, and which one the planner would pick. That is still only what the drone's own sensors support; nothing from the future. Second, it starts life as a copy of the planner, and with the forecast the copy matches the planner on every single decision. Third, it practises by trial and error, and every 10 rounds we test it on a separate set of tuning flights and keep the best version. Three lessons made practice work. One: when three buttons do the same thing, the policy's preference between them drifts, and it drifted to "land anyway", which bit it when someone stood at the pad; now it only sees options that are actually different. Two: tying it tightly to the planner stopped the drift but also stopped all learning. Three: a trained policy gives odds, not one answer, and there are three ways to fly it. The dice version can blend options to make a speed the menu does not have, like a gentle climb, and flying the odds-weighted average keeps that benefit without any randomness.
