This is the drone every Gazebo result from here on uses. One rig does both jobs, birds and obstacles. At the front, an OAK-D Lite: its colour camera is the best bird camera on the drone, and its depth camera feeds the voxel map. Three wide cameras look left, right and back from under the body plate. The first rig had its cameras on a mast above the propellers, but a mast makes the drone taller and gives the magpie a higher target, so they moved underneath. The camera on the top plate looking up was added after Sai asked about adding a camera: it doubles how much of the sky above the drone is seen, from 41 to 82 percent. The landing camera hangs on PX4's own gimbal and is held straight down. Three checks at the bottom. First, the camera model is right to about a pixel: I compared where each camera should see the bird with where it actually appears in the pictures. Second, the price of mounting under the plate: the spinning propellers fill about a quarter of each side picture, so detections there are thrown away. Third, Sai asked whether the gimbal was the wrong choice. In simulation it made no difference to landing accuracy: six landings each, 0.37 metres with the gimbal and 0.29 with a fixed downward camera, a tie within the wobble that the next slides fix. A real gimbal may still earn its keep in wind or vibration; the simulator cannot tell us that.
