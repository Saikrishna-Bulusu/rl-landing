The four flights side by side, all re-flown on 27 Sept with the new route following, the faster planner, the magpie's commit below 5 metres and, for the magpie, the retrained detector. In every one GPS was switched off over the suburb and stayed off to touchdown; the backup that would switch it back on never fired. All four landed on the pad: 6 centimetres in calm air with the rule, 19 in gusty wind with the flying learner, 4 past the unmapped crane with the rule and the local planner, and 4 with the magpie. The error column is how far PX4's own estimate was from the truth while GPS was off, a median over the whole flight: about two metres, set by GeoAnchor at cruise; on the pad marker it falls to ten or twenty centimetres. Still one flight per hazard: this shows the system can do it, not how often.
