The obstacle problem was a marker-size problem

A marker can only be decoded from h = size · f / min_px. At 1.20 m that is about 19 m — below a mature tree. So the aircraft is forced to hunt for the pad from inside the canopy layer, which is the one place flying is dangerous. The trees were never the problem; the ceiling was.

The pad grew a rung

Three markers side by side on a 5.0 m mat — nesting is impossible, a centre hole destroys the outer marker's data bits. The smallest sits on the model origin, so it is the touchdown point.

The marker does the work, not the search altitude. Raising the search altitude alongside it looked obviously right and bought nothing — +8.9 s, p = 0.12. Holding it fixed, the marker alone is worth −52 s, paired p = 0.009: the coarse rung is acquired during transit at cruise, so the search phase never runs at all.
