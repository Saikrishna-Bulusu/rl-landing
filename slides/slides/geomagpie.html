The magpie flight, re-flown. The learner trained with birds decides the landing, and it knows the magpie only through the camera rig. You spotted the two problems in the last film, and both are real. First, below 5 metres it kept hovering and looking for the magpie; now, once it is locked on its own pad and under 5 metres, it commits: a steady 0.4 metres per second down, centring on the way, no more waiting or dodging. In this flight the last 5 metres took 13 seconds at a constant 0.38. Second, the rig's detector fires on tree crowns. It found the magpie in 44 percent of its frame sets within 40 metres this time, against 10 percent last flight, but that number moves with where the bird happens to fly, and the false boxes on trees are still there, because the detector was trained on plain Gazebo ground, not on the photo. The fix is to retrain it with photo backgrounds and trees as negatives; that is tonight's job. It landed 10 centimetres from the centre after three swoops, the closest 1.9 metres, with GPS denied for 116 seconds.
