The magpie flight, re-flown this evening with the retrained detector. The learner trained with birds decides the landing and knows the magpie only through the camera rig. Two things you pointed out are fixed. Below 5 metres, once locked on its own pad, it commits: a steady 0.37 metres per second down, centring on the way, with no hovering or dodging. And the rig no longer mistakes tree crowns for magpies: in this flight's landing only 18 percent of its magpie alarms were phantoms, against 54 percent with the old detector; the next slide shows why. What did not change is how often the rig truly sees the bird, about a fifth of the time it is within 40 metres, because the magpie attacks from above and behind, where this rig has no camera. It landed 4 centimetres from the centre after three swoops, the closest 2 metres, with GPS denied for 116 seconds.
