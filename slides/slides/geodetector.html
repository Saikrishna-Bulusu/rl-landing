You said the cameras kept seeing trees as magpies, and you were right. The rig's detector had only ever been trained on pictures of the plain Gazebo ground, so over the photo floor it boxed tree crowns and roofs. Tonight it was retrained: the same bench rig placed at 800 new spots over the photo floor, the magpie put around it at 2 to 70 metres, and two empty views at every spot so the trees are there as examples of what is not a magpie. On held-out pictures the old detector drew a false box in more than every other picture, 0.57 per picture; the retrained one in one picture in five hundred, and it also finds more magpies, 81 percent against 75. On the old plain ground it is as good as before. In the re-flown magpie landing, 54 percent of the old rig's magpie alarms had been phantoms; now 18 percent. The real sightings did not change, about a fifth of the time the bird is within 40 metres, and that is the rig's blind sky above and behind, not the detector. One small cost: very far birds, 45 to 70 metres, are found a bit less often.
