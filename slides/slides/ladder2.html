Rule-break column: corridor world; random 3.4%, 1.1%, 0.3%.

Before training anything, the same test as in part one: is there anything to learn? Three decision-makers flew the same 4,096 flights. The rule is the hand-written baseline. The planner uses exactly the drone's sensors but thinks 20 seconds ahead about every option. The oracle is handed each flight's real future, where every helicopter, drone and person will be, and plans with that. The planner beats the rule by about 2 points and cuts rule-breaking from 3.8% of flights to 1.4%, so better decisions alone are worth something, and that makes the planner the real bar for any learned policy. The second card is a correction I have to own: at the time I called the oracle a ceiling and said about 3% of failures were unavoidable. That was wrong. The oracle only looks 20 seconds ahead, and a trained policy later beat it, so it bounds 20-second planners, not everything.
