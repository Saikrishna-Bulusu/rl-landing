The obstacle flight of 27 Sept: GPS on for the climb, then denied to touchdown. GeoAnchor unmodified; PX4 v1.16.2 in Gazebo.

How the pieces fit in one flight. The drone climbs on GPS, and once GeoAnchor's fixes are arriving steadily over the suburb, GPS is switched off for the rest of the flight. From then on the downward camera's pictures go through GeoAnchor, its own unmodified pipeline and its accept-or-reject gate, and every accepted fix goes into PX4's position estimator as an auxiliary position. Below 50 metres GeoAnchor fades, so the pad marker takes its place as the position source, and a downward rangefinder gives the height. The chart is one real flight. Blue is how far PX4's own estimate was from where the drone really was. On GPS it is near zero. On GeoAnchor it wanders between one and eight metres, median about 2.7. The spike to 10 metres is the hand-over at 50 metres, just before the marker comes into view. Once the marker is read the error drops to about ten centimetres and stays there to touchdown.
