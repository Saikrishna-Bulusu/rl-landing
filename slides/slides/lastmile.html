Delivery into a real suburb, under real rules

Nobody puts a delivery pad in a forest. So the question moved to where delivery drones actually fly: the final descent into a back yard in Logan, Queensland, sharing the sky with an air ambulance, other delivery drones and the people on the ground.

Part two starts here. The first part of the study ended with a forest, and the honest criticism is that no real delivery goes into a forest. So the setting changed to the last kilometre of a real delivery: a 4 by 4 km patch of Logan in south-east Queensland, built from 4,582 real building footprints (Microsoft's open data plus OpenStreetMap). Every house with a suitable back yard is a possible pad: 1,124 of them. Deliveries start at the Palms Shopping Centre, which stands in for a delivery hub. Logan Hospital sits in the middle with a real helipad, so the air ambulance is a real neighbour, and the dashed circle is the 1.4 km zone where Australian rules say a drone must give way to a helicopter. The point of the map: every result from here on is measured in this suburb, not in a toy world.
